# Plan: W4, the interaction Kit and the V3 benches rebuilt as Kit components

> Roadmap: `refdocs/plans/2026-10-22-completion-roadmap.md` (W4). Written after the first stage was built; kept true to what happened (CLAUDE.md rules 2 and 4). No paid call is made by anything here.

## Why

Synapse V3's four benches (Hooke's law with draggable masses, titration with a stopcock and an eye-level reading, yeast fermentation with tubes and a dial, an orbit scene) are raw HTML strings with their own palettes and unchecked physics. The demo video shows them. To be shown in V4 they must be built from **Kit micro-components with cited, independently referenced models**, and the verifier must **drive the gestures** (D-17: verification presses real input), or they cannot carry any label above `model-checked`.

## Stages (one at a time; the next starts when the previous is green end to end)

| Stage | Bench | New Kit parts | New model | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hooke's law: hang masses on a spring | `massHanger` (control), `springScene` (scene), the audit's `widgets` report, the slider `snap` option | none (`mechanics.hookes` exists) | **Done 2026-10-24** (this plan, section "Stage 1") |
| 2 | Titration: stopcock, burette, eye level | `valve` (control), `titrationScene` (burette, flask, eye) | a new cited end-point model, `chemistry.titration_endpoint_volume` (`chemistry.titration` exists) | **Done 2026-10-25** (section "Stage 2") |
| 3 | Yeast fermentation: tubes and a dial | `dial` (control), `fermentationScene` (tubes, limewater, thermometers) | a new cited energy model, `biology.respiration_energy` (not a rate: no textbook page gives one) | **Done 2026-10-25** (section "Stage 3") |
| 4 | Orbit (Newton's cannonball) | `orbitScene` (a plain slider is the control) | two cited models, `physics.circular_speed` and `physics.escape_speed` (Physics Form 5, Gravitation) | **Built 2026-10-25, review owed** (section "Stage 4") |

Each stage gets: a written precondition and accessibility fence per component, a registration suite that refuses every planted defect, a harness drive of the gesture, citations from the local corpus for any new model, an independent hand-written reference, L6 rows, and a `kit-guardian` review before the relock is committed.

## Stage 1 design

- **`massHanger(env, {id,label,unit,min,max,value,grid})`** is a control with a different surface. It is built on `slider`, so the labelled range input is still on the page, still `data-synapse-control`, and every sweep, provenance and reference check is a slider's. `grid` is the number of masses (2 to 8). The masses are Kit action buttons (a letter, never a number) that can be dragged (pointer events, pointer captured: mouse, pen, touch) or pressed (Enter, Space, a tap). The control snaps every value to a whole number of masses, so a script cannot set half a mass.
- **`springScene(env, {extension, full})`** draws a spring whose length follows the extension. It accepts only the output of `mechanics.hookes`, writes words only (no number), and is held to its drawing by the audit.
- **The audit's `widgets` report** is how a composite widget answers for itself, in two halves. **Structure** (cheap, no false alarms, also acted on by the student's runtime self-check): each mass is in the part the control says, not hidden or restyled by attribute, the Tray and Hanger captions are Kit-owned text, the scene's polyline, pan, label and stroke are what the Kit drew, and it is still on the stage. **Layout** (read from a real layout, in view; acted on only by the verifier): the bench and its parts have a usable size, each mass sits inside its part, is not faded and is not covered by another element, and the spring's pan sits where the extension puts it at the picture's real scale. Any problem is a refusal (`widget_tampered`, L1-022). The verifier also holds the scene to the plan: its chain counts as a model use (`plan_mismatch` for a second instance), its input units are checked, and its `full` scale must fit the model (`scene_scale`, L1-024). A scene not drawing `mechanics.hookes` is `scene_ungrounded` (L1-023).
- **The harness (`api/verify/render.py` `_drive_drags`)** takes what must exist from the audit, never from page markers. It drags every mass onto the hanger with the mouse (each must raise the control by exactly one step and move the readouts), drags every mass off, presses every mass from the keyboard (Enter to hang, Space to take off), and drags every mass onto and off the hanger with a finger (CDP touch). A dead or blocked mass is `drag_dead` (L2-006). A Playwright failure is a harness error (fail closed), not evidence about the masses.
- **Plan fields and checks.** `ControlSpec.surface` (`slider` default, or `masses`) and `Plan.scene` (`kind: spring`, `source`, `full`). PLAN-018: a masses control needs 2 to 8 masses and a start on a whole number of masses. PLAN-019: a scene must draw a model instance of an allowed model, and its `full` scale must lie between the model's output at the controls' maximum and twice that.
- **Not a new trust claim.** A mass bench is `kit-verified` only under the existing rule (a textbook citation); the bench adds no citation of its own.

## Prompt change

The Plan schema grew two fields, the Hooke exemplar now carries masses and a scene, and the planner now receives the schema as compact JSON (the file stays indented for review). The pack is versioned `planner.v3` (same words as v2) so a later measurement names the request it measured. The planner request had reached the 7000-token cap; compacting removes about 40 percent of the schema's tokens with no change in content. This is a `prompt:` change (CLAUDE.md rule 7). **Eval delta: none measured.** No paid planner run was made (fakes are the default and a paid run needs the owner's go); the offline request-size projection was regenerated (`refdocs/evidence/2026-10-16-p3-cost-projection.md`). The first live planner canary after this (W8) is the delta, and it must be compared with the 2026-10-18 no-exemplar result (3 of 3 at first attempt).

## Out of scope for stage 1

The titration, yeast and orbit benches; Bahasa Melayu strings for the new components (W6); the Amali record path (W5); any change to how the web hosts a frame.

## Known limits (recorded, not hidden)

- **A pointer-transparent sheet laid over the bench** hides the masses without disturbing hit testing; the audit reads layout, not pixels. A recorded L6 known miss (`drag-overlay-pointer-transparent`); only a pixel check would catch it.
- The student's self-check acts on structure only, so a style rule that moves a mass in a student's browser is caught only if the verifier saw it first (pages are assembled from the Plan, so the verified page is the page).
- The verifier checks the light theme only.
- The runtime self-check re-audits on every pointer move (pre-existing cost, now with two more checks).
- On a phone the controls sit below the stage, so the masses and the spring are far apart; a layout choice, not a defect of the checks.

## Stage 2 design: the titration bench

- **`valve(env, {id,label,unit,min,max,value,grid})`** is a control with a stopcock surface, built on `slider` with `snap`, exactly as `massHanger` is. The student holds the stopcock to run the liquid in (one step every 140 ms after a 250 ms hold), taps it, or presses it from the keyboard (Enter or Space); letting go, losing focus or reaching the limit stops the flow. The range input stays for the keyboard and screen readers. `grid` is the number of steps (2 to 40). The stopcock is a registered Kit action, a real button of at least 44 px with a words-only name and a polite live region ("Stopcock open: the liquid is running.").
- **`titrationScene(env, {added, endpoint, full})`** draws a burette over a flask and gives the student an eye to read the meniscus. `added` must be a control in cm3 (the valve's), `endpoint` must be the output of the new end-point model, `full` is the burette's capacity (at least the control's maximum). The burette empties as `added` rises; the flask is colourless until `added` reaches `endpoint`, then pink. The eye is a Kit button dragged up and down the burette (or moved by the arrow keys, Home and End); a Kit-owned sentence says in words whether the eye is level with the meniscus or above or below it, and to bring it level before reading. **The scene prints no number and reads none from the eye.** The sentence says only what the textbook says (page 171: keep the eye level with the meniscus; pointer `titr_eye_level`). It does not say which way a wrong position misreads: V3's bench said "above reads too high", which is backwards for a burette (its scale rises downward, so an eye above the meniscus reads too low), and the textbook states neither direction. A modelled parallax would be an invented quantity with no source.
- **`chemistry.titration_endpoint_volume`** (`Vb = Ma x Va x b / (a x Mb)`) is the existing titration relation read the other way: given the alkali's concentration, the volume that neutralises the acid. It is a Kit science model with a precondition, units, a hand-computed reference, properties, a registration suite with eleven planted bugs, and an independent Python reference (`api/verify/references.py`). The two models are each other's inverse and a test checks that. Its citation is the two Form 4 pages the titration Plan already cites (`titr_formula`, `titr_endpoint`), whose pointers now list it.
- **Why the end point is derived, not typed.** The indicator's colour change is the lesson, so where it happens must be the model's number, never a number the Plan or the page wrote. The Plan holds the alkali's true concentration as a constant, the end-point model turns it into a volume, and the scene compares the control with that volume. The control's lowest position is 5 cm3 (the concentration model is undefined at 0).
- **Plan.** `ControlSpec.surface` gains `valve`; `SceneSpec.kind` gains `titration` with an `endpoint` field (required for a titration, forbidden for a spring). PLAN-018 now covers valves (2 to 40 steps, a start on a step). PLAN-019 for a titration: the source is a control in cm3, the burette holds the control's range and is at most twice it, the endpoint is an instance of the end-point model, and the end point lies inside the control's range (otherwise the student could never see the colour change). The Plan's steps are in V4 wording (hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide; the V3 bench's HNO3 and KOH are not copied).
- **The verifier.** The scene's end-point chain is a model use (`plan_mismatch`), its input units are checked, its source chain must be a control in cm3, and the burette's capacity and the end point are checked against the control (`scene_scale`, L1-024). The structural and layout halves follow D-44: the liquid, the meniscus line, the flask's colour, the description, the eye's position, the eye's sentence and the parts' places are held to the numbers; layout checks the picture is drawn, not faded, the meniscus and the eye are where the numbers put them, and nothing covers the eye.
- **The harness.** `_drive_valves`: for each valve the Kit reports, a mouse tap lets out exactly one step, Enter and Space each one step, a mouse hold runs at least two steps and stops when released, and a finger does a tap and a hold; a stopcock that keeps running after release is refused; extra stopcocks the Kit did not report are refused (`valve_dead`, L2-007). `_drive_eyes`: with the control high, the keyboard sends the eye to the top (the sentence must say above) and the bottom (below); the mouse and a finger each drag it to the top (above), the meniscus (level) and the bottom (below); a dead eye is `eye_dead` (L2-008).

### Stage 2 limits (recorded, not hidden)

- The eye teaches a reading habit in words. It does not change any number, so a student who reads from the wrong place is told to bring the eye level, not corrected by the readout. It is a button moved by the arrow keys; a screen reader in browse mode may read the arrows as cursor moves (a `role=slider` with word values would fix that; a screen-reader pass is still owed).
- The indicator is one colour change at one volume (a sharp end point); a gradual change near the end point, and a drop-by-drop final approach, are not modelled.
- Layout is checked only in the light theme and at the harness's viewport; a pointer-transparent overlay is still an L6 known miss (D-44).

## Stage 3 design: the fermentation bench

- **`dial(env, {id,label,unit,min,max,value,grid})`** is a control turned like a knob, built on `slider` with `snap`. The pointer's angle (clockwise from straight up) sets the value, from the first mark at the lower left to the last at the lower right; the gap at the bottom belongs to the nearer end. The range input stays on the page and is the keyboard and screen-reader path (arrow keys, Home, End); the knob is a pointer surface and is `aria-hidden`, so there is one control for assistive technology, not two.
- **`fermentationScene(env, {fermented, energy, full})`** draws the textbook's Activity 7.3 apparatus. `fermented` is a control in mol; `energy` must be `biology.respiration_energy` fed by that same control and must be alcohol fermentation's 210 kJ per mole (the picture is false for any other pathway). As soon as glucose is fermented, tube A bubbles (a few, steady, plenty: in words), its limewater clouds and its thermometer reads higher than tube B's, which, as the control, shows none of it. It prints no number.
- **`biology.respiration_energy`**: E = n x e, with e = 210 kJ/mol (alcohol fermentation), 150 (lactic acid fermentation) or 2898 (aerobic respiration), from Table 7.1 (Form 4 Biology, printed page 121) and the alcohol fermentation equation (printed page 118). The textbook says "per molecule"; the model reads per mole (ASSUMED, D-46). Registration suite with ten planted bugs, a hand-computed reference, an independent Python reference, properties, and the pointers `ferment_products`, `ferment_energy` and `ferment_activity` (the apparatus and the activity's variables, printed page 120).
- **The Plan** (`data/plans/biology-f4-fermentation.json`, a 17th golden Plan): a dial for the glucose fermented in tube A (0 to 0.004 mol in eight steps), two instances of the energy model (fermentation, aerobic), two readouts, two graphs, a results table, a prediction card, a concept check, the textbook's variables on the experiment card, and a sentence stating 210 and 2898 kJ per mole.
- **What is not built from V3:** the setup drags and the timer (D-46).
- **Verification.** `_drive_dials` turns each dial by mouse and finger to the middle, the top and the bottom, starting at the first mark, then presses real arrow keys on its range input and reads the Kit's own value from the audit. The verifier refuses a scene for another pathway, a page-made drawing, text added inside the scene, a page that swaps which energy feeds which readout (`binding_problems`), and one that blocks trusted input events.

### Stage 3 limits (recorded, not hidden)

- The thermometer and the limewater are two-state; the energy model scales a table value, so what the bench teaches is the comparison (about seven percent of the aerobic energy) and the products, not a new relation.
- The knob has no accessible role of its own (its range input does the work); no screen-reader pass has been done.
- Layout is checked only in the light theme and at one viewport.


## Stage 4 design: the orbit bench (2026-10-25, built in the cloud with no corpus and no reviewer)

**What it is.** Newton's cannonball: a student sets the launch speed of a cannonball fired sideways from a very high mountain, and sees whether it falls back to Earth, circles it, goes round in an oval, or escapes. V3 did this with a canvas simulation whose physics nobody checked. Here the lesson's numbers are two cited models and the picture is a Kit scene held to them.

- **Control.** A plain `slider` in km/s (no new control part). The slider's own sweep, provenance and reference checks apply unchanged.
- **Models.** Three, all Physics Form 4 (Circular Motion and Gravitation). `physics.circular_speed`, v = sqrt(G M / r), and `physics.escape_speed`, v = sqrt(2 G M / r), both with the radius as an input (km, from a declared constant), the central mass as a parameter (kg) and the output in km/s. G is fixed inside the model (6.674e-11 N m2/kg2). Each has a precondition, units and domain, a hand-computed reference, properties, a registration suite of planted bugs and an independent Python reference (`api/verify/references.py`). The two are tied: escape speed is exactly the square root of 2 times the circular speed at the same radius, and a test checks that. **The third model was added when the harness refused the first build** (`dead_control`: the launch speed moved the picture but no readout, because both speeds depend only on the radius): `physics.centripetal_acceleration`, a = v^2 / r (speed km/s, radius km, result m/s2). It gives the launch speed a readout, and at the circular speed it equals the pull of gravity at that radius, which a test checks.
- **Scene.** `orbitScene(env, {speed, circular, escape, full})` draws Earth, the mountain and the launch arrow (its length follows `speed / full`), and a path chosen by comparing the speed with the two model outputs: below the circular speed the cannonball falls back; within 2 percent of it, a circle; between the circular and the escape speed, an oval that grows as the speed rises; at or above the escape speed, a path that leaves. A Kit-owned sentence says which, in words only; the scene prints no number. The drawing is a schematic and says so in words ("not to scale; the mountain is drawn far taller than a real one").
- **Pinned to Earth.** Like the fermentation scene's pathway, the scene's words are about Earth, so the Plan check requires the central mass to be Earth's and the radius to be Earth's radius (a constant in km). (The PLAN-020 constants table is not used for this: it would refuse another planet's mass for the same models outside a scene.)
- **Plan.** `SceneSpec.kind` gains `orbit`, with `source` (the control in km/s), `model` (the circular-speed instance) and a new `escape` (the escape-speed instance); `full` is the speed, in km/s, drawn as the arrow's full length. PLAN-019 for an orbit: the control is in km/s; `full` is at least the control's maximum and at most twice it; both instances are of the right models, fed by the same constant, with Earth's mass; the control's range must include the circular speed and the escape speed (otherwise the student could never see two of the four outcomes).
- **The verifier.** The scene's two chains are model uses (`plan_mismatch`), the source chain must be a control in km/s, both models must be fed by the same declared constant (`scene_ungrounded`), and the structural and layout halves follow D-44 to D-46: the exact attributes of every part, the elements inside the picture and the text holders are held by the guard; the sentence and the description follow the numbers; layout checks the picture is drawn and not faded.
- **The harness.** No new gesture: the control is a slider, which the harness already sweeps. The sweep must cross all four outcomes for a Plan to pass (checked at Plan time by PLAN-019 and re-checked by the verifier from the sentence).

### Stage 4 limits (recorded, not hidden)

- **No corpus, no review.** The models' citations stay `UNVERIFIED (corpus not loaded until P2)` like every model before the corpus; the pointers file names the Gravitation pages to be found and quoted locally. A model-checked Environment is the most this stage can mint in the cloud. No `kit-guardian` pass was run (the owner has not asked for one this session); the first two stages each needed two rounds, so expect findings.
- The path is a schematic, not an integrated trajectory; the drawn oval's shape follows the speed ratio but its size is not to scale. The launch is always tangential from the top.
- A launch from the surface of Earth is used for the numbers (7.9 and 11.2 km/s), so the "mountain" is a teaching exaggeration, which the words say.
- Layout is checked only in the light theme; a pointer-transparent overlay is still an L6 known miss.
